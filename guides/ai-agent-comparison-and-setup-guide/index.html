Which AI agent fits your workflow?

A quick comparison of Muse, ChatGPT dots, and Grok Bot, plus the first steps to make one useful.

By Digital Tilda · October 2026 · 5 min read

Compare the agentsSet up your agentCopy the task briefSources

01 / Choose your agent

Choose the job first.

Pick one task you repeat every week. Give an agent a clear outcome, relevant sources, and a point where it should ask for your review.

At a glance
AgentGood fit if…What stands outTry this first
MuseYou run a small business or spend a lot of time in Meta’s apps.Simple messaging interface; connects to Instagram professional analytics, Facebook Pages, Meta ad accounts, and other business tools.Ask it to review recent social performance and draft next week’s content ideas.
ChatGPT dotsYour projects, ideas, and context already live in ChatGPT.An ongoing agent that uses your ChatGPT context and connected apps, and keeps working between conversations.Give it ownership of a weekly content pipeline and ask for drafts to review.
Grok BotYou want distinct agents for distinct roles.Named Bots can work in parallel, coordinate, and build up context around each job.Create one research Bot to find and brief five content ideas.

The decision in one sentence

Start with Muse for connected business tasks, dots for ongoing work already rooted in ChatGPT, or Grok Bot when separate specialist roles would help.

These are starting points, not performance rankings. Test one real task before giving an agent more access or a recurring responsibility.

02 / From sign-up to first result

Set up your first agent

Use the path for the agent you chose. Keep the first task small enough to review in one sitting.

Muse

Best first test: a connected business task

Open Muse ↗
  1. Open muse.ai or the Muse app and sign in. Muse is currently available in the US and Canada.
  2. Describe one goal and the finished result you want. Start with a draft or analysis you can check.
  3. In Muse settings, connect only the apps needed for that task; review the access each connection requests.
  4. Review the output before asking Muse to take on an ongoing workflow. Muse asks before publishing, sending, or spending.

ChatGPT dots

Best first test: an ongoing project

Open ChatGPT dots ↗
  1. On desktop, open chatgpt.com/dots or the ChatGPT desktop app and follow onboarding. Access depends on plan and rollout.
  2. Name your dot and give it one clear project goal, useful context, and the output you want back.
  3. Connect the apps it needs and review permissions before widening access.
  4. Give it a review point: what it may do independently, and when it should return to you for a decision.

Grok Bot

Best first test: a named specialist

Get Grok Bot ↗
  1. Get Grok Bot from x.ai/bot and sign in with an eligible paid Cursor plan or linked SuperGrok plan.
  2. Create your first Bot. Give it a short name, one primary job, and a description of how it should work.
  3. Connect a supported app or sign in to a website when the Bot asks you to take over.
  4. Assign a task with an outcome, sources, constraints, deliverable, and review point. Add another Bot only when you have a distinct second role.

03 / Make it useful

Copy this first-task brief

Goal: [the result I want]. Use: [the files, apps, or sources]. Deliver: [a draft, list, report, or completed artifact] by [time]. Boundaries: Ask me before sending, publishing, purchasing, or changing anything external. Show your sources, flag uncertainty, and tell me what decision you need from me.

Make the details yours before you use it.
