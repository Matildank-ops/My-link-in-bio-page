A note before you begin

The ChatGPT Prompt Codes Field Guide

The video calls phrases such as “80/20,” “truth mode,” and “red team” secret codes. They are useful shorthand for instructions you write in a prompt; they do not activate hidden ChatGPT modes. This guide gives you the ten methods shown in the video and 200 complete variations.

For each example, replace the bracketed context. Then inspect the result, especially any factual claim, quote or source. A sharp prompt improves the request; it cannot guarantee a correct answer.

Chapter 012 min read

How to use the ten lenses

Start with a real task, a concrete outcome and the material ChatGPT should use. Then add one lens. If you need two perspectives, ask for one at a time and compare the answers.

  1. 80/20. Identify the few actions most likely to drive the result. Separate evidence from guesses, rank actions by impact and effort, and name the first action.
  2. Socratic. Teach by asking one question at a time. Wait for my answer; correct misunderstandings gently and give a final synthesis after we work through it.
  3. Truth check. Challenge my assumptions directly. Identify missing evidence, a plausible alternative, and a test that could prove me wrong.
  4. Lindy. Favor methods with a long track record. Explain what has endured, what is new, and where the older method may fail.
  5. Explain simply. Explain this for a bright 10-year-old. Define jargon, use one concrete example, and then add the adult nuance.
  6. Future self. Respond as a thoughtful version of me one year from now who achieved the goal ethically. Focus on decisions I can make today, not pretend predictions.
  7. Red team. Take the strongest opposing view. List failure modes, early warning signs, and mitigations without inventing facts.
  8. Natural voice. Rewrite this in a natural voice with specific nouns and varied sentence length. Preserve the facts and do not add claims.
  9. Unlearn. Identify beliefs here that may be outdated or unsupported. For each, show what evidence would update the belief.
  10. Offer review. Analyze the offer as a practical business coach: audience, problem, proof, friction, price logic, and a small test. Avoid imitating a named person.

Example: “I am choosing between two newsletter topics. My audience is [description], my evidence is [data], and I can run one two-week test. Use the 80/20 lens and propose that test.”

The “Hormozi” reference in the clip is treated here as an offer-review lens rather than a request to impersonate a living person.
